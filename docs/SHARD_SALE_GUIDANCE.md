# Shard sale guidance

## Status and scope

In local development for Minecraft **26.1.2 only**, under the approved design and plan in `docs/superpowers/`. No release, GitHub publication or installation has been requested. The intended feature is an opt-in three-hour advisory for Safari shards in the Hunting Box and passive, local warnings about previously observed personal sell offers. This does not restore the shelved valuation feature.

## Verified data contract

Current prices use the public [Hypixel Bazaar endpoint](https://api.hypixel.net/): the lowest valid `buy_summary` price is the competing sell-offer ask; the highest valid `sell_summary` price is the bid. Existing `/profit` weighted `quick_status` prices and ranking retain their existing meaning.

Historical context provisionally uses [SkyCofl's documented public history API](https://sky.coflnet.com/wiki/api), with [data attribution](https://sky.coflnet.com/data). Its [legal page](https://sky.coflnet.com/about) asks API users to link that data page. Only documented anonymous GET endpoints are used; authenticated exports and personal-order APIs are excluded. Source inspection is evidence of field semantics, not copied implementation code.

The public SkyApi `Controllers/BazaarController.cs` passes UTC ranges to SkyBazaar. SkyBazaar `Services/BazaarService.cs` replaces snapshot `BuyPrice` with the first sell-offer ask before aggregation where the book is present. `Controllers/BazaarController.cs` exposes that representative price as `buy`, and timestamps are UTC (including timezone-less serialized UTC values). A timestamped Hideonwall snapshot cross-check matched `buy` to its lowest `buyOrders` offer, 155341.8 coins. However, historical graphs may fall back to quick-status prices when order books are absent, without identifying which observations used that fallback. **This graph source remains unqualified for automatic advice.** Parsed records carry unverified price provenance; a coherent spread alone cannot establish comparability with a live best ask.

The history resolution is chosen by **range length**: less than one hour uses detailed snapshots; less than 24 hours uses minute aggregates; a 24-hour or seven-day request uses coarser aggregates. Therefore replay collection uses bounded public ranges shorter than 24 hours, with overlap removed. A tested 23-hour range two days in the past returned 471 samples; an exactly 24-hour range returned 12. Weekly data is context, never a fabricated fine-grained replay. Normalize observations into closed five-minute market bins; never use intrabin maximum/minimum to assert an earlier target touch. Bin close is **not** proof of publication availability. Keep market observation, bin close and actual delivery time separate; missing delivery evidence prevents deployment-equivalent validation.

The catalogue survey returned HTTP 200 and 81–82 weekly samples for all 37 Safari shard IDs. This establishes availability, not model accuracy, freshness or eligibility. The sample day-history response was approximately 26 minutes behind collection time, exceeding the proposed 15-minute history freshness bound; such data must remain unknown until a sufficiently fresh provider/local observation exists. Do not silently weaken that bound.

Replay needs earlier context before its evaluation window. Fetch and process older data chronologically in bounded chunks, keeping only the rolling cache plus a small margin in memory. The full downloaded research evidence stays outside the product/repository. Do not retain an unbounded historical collection in the mod.

## Validation

Unmodified 26.1.2 baseline: 1,139 tests passed with an external Gradle init script pointing Java's temporary directory to the feature's isolated local test directory. Initial normal-temp runs had transient JUnit directory-cleanup failures (three, then one different test); behavioral assertions were not disabled, and affected tests also passed unchanged on a focused retry. Keep those failures in the local validation record.

The local feature branch has been rebased onto GitHub master `f46a7b6` (v4.0.1). The earlier research checkpoint passed 1,193 tests on 26.1.2. Additional adapter/cache/service regression results are retained in the local execution ledger; no other Minecraft targets are built. Independent review identified missing price-provenance and publication-time enforcement; both are covered by tests that contrast passing known-source synthetic series with unqualified references and delayed delivery. Retrospective price-series statistics may still be reported, but cannot enable guidance.

Across the ten shards in the user's reference document, the initial rule produced 44–47 usable non-overlapping three-hour research windows per product. Training HOLD-target touch rates were 0–33%, below the approved 60%; every held-out day contained only 0–4 HOLD decisions, below the required five. None of 24 fixed quantile/weight combinations passed training selection for any of these ten products. Selection used only the training prefix. These observations reject these candidates on this sample; they do not establish that every possible automatic method fails. They are retrospective price-series research, not observed execution or causal service validation.

The human subsequently approved expanding the final validation period to the most recent **three complete UTC days**, keeping the three-hour horizon and all other performance/evidence limits unchanged. Replay policy version 2 records that period explicitly and rejects an unfinished validation period. Research evidence was extended to 14 days of fine observations plus earlier coarse context. The original rule produced 87–95 usable windows per product, including 22–24 validation windows, but no product met all acceptance limits. On this extended sample, training selection across the same 24 fixed candidates found one statistically passing Bloodbat candidate; its validation set had no HOLD decisions, so it remains inconclusive and ineligible. The other nine products had no passing training candidate.

A separate exploratory movement-based model tested 36 fixed neighbor/quantile/risk combinations using only completed earlier three-hour examples. None passed training selection for the ten reference products. These are failed research candidates, not deployed advice; prior inspection of this historical period also prevents calling it an independent confirmatory test.

Alternative public sources initially did not resolve qualification: Skytrack's ingestion code stores weighted quick-status prices without actual receipt times; SkyMarket's seven-day response contains hourly observations. Skyblock Finance access was subsequently resolved: its current public symbol lookup uses exchange `bz` and tickers `bz!a!SHARD_HIDEONWALL` (instant buy/ask) and `bz!b!SHARD_HIDEONWALL` (instant sell/bid). Earlier UnsupportedSymbol responses came from the outdated `BAZAAR|ID/COINS-BUY` format in a stale client bundle. A bounded public history request returned 20,160 one-minute Hideonwall candles covering 14 complete UTC days, with no duplicate timestamps or missing minute steps. All prices are positive/finite and closing prices lie within their stated high/low. In 1,723 candles the open lies outside that range and equals the preceding close; qualify that chart convention before using opens or extrema. This establishes usable close-price research access, not accuracy of every historical candle or actual publication timing; no publication timestamps are supplied. One recent ask matched a recorded official best ask. Historical aggregation and model qualification remain pending, with no invented delivery timestamps.

The human initially approved a bounded prospective official-quote collection, then explicitly requested its shutdown in favor of past data. The [local Python collector](../tools/shard_market/README.md) is **stopped**, its worker exited, and six selected-quote snapshots are preserved. Do not restart it without a new instruction. The helper remains separate from mod runtime. Preserve server/local time differences for qualification rather than assuming synchronized clocks.

The historical audit subsequently cached all 20 ask/bid series for the ten reference products: 403,200 minute bars over the same 14 complete days. A common 622-minute interval has unchanged closes and null volume across all products. A complete timestamp grid is therefore not proof of fresh independent observations. Null/suspect intervals and crossed/missing quote pairs are excluded from replay, without local price repair or forward fill. The source files and profile stay outside the repository.

`FinanceHistoryParsing` accepts bounded one-minute chart responses, selects genuine closes for completed five-minute bins, excludes null-volume sampling flags and leaves provenance/availability explicitly unqualified. It never uses candle highs or chart volume as executed trades. The test-only JVM replay accepts explicitly identified Finance inputs; unsupported source identifiers fail rather than defaulting to another provider.

`MarketHistoryStore` prunes to eight days, 2,304 fine and 96 coarse points per allowed product, with at most 37 IDs. Compact final/staging files are each capped at 8 MiB, together at 16 MiB. Model/schema mismatches and malformed/oversized files are rejected; atomic replacement preserves the previous cache if a write fails. The isolated `ShardMarketService` core coalesces client demand onto one supplied worker, serializes historical requests with at least one elapsed second between starts, handles refresh/backoff/cancellation and rejects late generations. It uses the shared current-quote throttle and publishes cached immutable recommendations. Reads can remove stale advice without doing network or disk work. Calibration identifiers reset state/alert baselines. Provider HTTP binding and runtime registration are still pending; none of this starts a replacement background collector.

The current feature is **incomplete**. No Hunting Box highlights, order watching or chat alerts have been enabled. The human chose continued automatic-model research; no Google Doc-target fallback is authorized. A qualified source and a passing model are needed before dependent integration tasks. Target touches do not establish order fills. No automatic buying, selling, cancellation or repricing is permitted. No source changes have been pushed or installed.

Research runs in three local chats grouped under the human-requested **Sparkling Mutuals research** project: source audit, three-hour model research and a final modest-move check. The fixed 36-candidate 2%–6% gain family and the 12-candidate 0.6%–1% gain family found no training-qualified product on the filtered sample. The modest-move family's maximum training touch rate was 4/9 (44.4%). These reject those families, not every possible model. The original 24-candidate production-model replay found a training-passing Chuckwalla candidate but only two validation HOLDs, below the required five; no product qualified overall. Only 11–13 of 24 validation windows remain usable per product after coverage checks around the suspect gap. No thresholds, dates or source-evidence requirements were relaxed to force a result.
